## Origins

macOS descends from NeXTSTEP, the Unix-based system Apple acquired with NeXT in 1996 — the grey NeXTcube desktop that made that system is running elsewhere in this gallery. Mac OS X arrived publicly in 2001, joining the Mach- and BSD-derived Darwin foundation and XNU kernel to the Aqua graphical interface and Macintosh application traditions. Over two decades, that architecture moved from PowerPC to Intel processors and then to Apple-designed ARM64 systems without abandoning the Finder-centered desktop identity.

macOS Sequoia, released in September 2024, represents that contemporary Apple-silicon era; the name comes from the national park of giant trees, continuing the line of Californian place names Apple moved to with Monterey. Finder, the Dock, Spotlight, Safari, Terminal, and system frameworks coexist with Continuity features that connect Macs to iPhones, iPads, watches, and cloud services. The interface remains recognizably descended from Mac OS X even as its controls and application model increasingly align with Apple’s other platforms.

## Significance

The modern Mac is a union of several histories: classic Macintosh attention to graphical interaction, NeXT’s object-oriented development environment, open Unix standards, proprietary frameworks, and tightly integrated hardware. That combination made the platform influential in publishing, software development, media production, and consumer computing while sustaining a smaller but unusually cohesive ecosystem.

Apple’s ARM transition is especially consequential. By controlling processor design, operating system, compilers, and product hardware, the company could reshape performance and power characteristics together. Rosetta 2 preserved many Intel applications during the transition, demonstrating compatibility as a planned migration tool rather than permanent architectural stasis.

## Legacy

Sequoia belongs to an unfinished chapter, but macOS already demonstrates exceptional architectural continuity across radical processor change. The XNU and Aqua settlement formed in 2001 still frames the platform, while Apple silicon has altered its relationship to the wider PC market. macOS links early graphical systems to a present desktop where hardware, software, and services are designed as one controlled whole.
