## Origins

The **Spectravideo SVI-728**, introduced in 1984, brought the company into the **MSX** home-computer standard. Spectravideo's earlier SV-328 had its own BASIC and hardware arrangement. The 728 retained the keyboard-computer form while joining a platform shared with machines from other manufacturers.

Its Z80 processor, 64 KiB of main memory and 16 KiB of video memory supported colour graphics and programmable sound. Microsoft's MSX BASIC lived in ROM, ready at power-on. The owner's manual began with first programs and worked through loops, decisions, graphics and music: learning the machine meant learning to tell it what to do.

## Significance

MSX made the software platform larger than the name on the case. A common BASIC environment and defined hardware interfaces gave software and expansion makers a target shared by multiple computer brands. For Spectravideo, moving from the SV-328 to the SVI-728 meant participating in that wider system.

BASIC exposed much of the hardware directly. Commands such as `SCREEN`, `COLOR` and `PLAY` let a short program change the display and produce music. The prompt was therefore a starting point for experiments with pictures and sound as well as arithmetic. This exhibit's small listing uses that combination: a greeting, a table of numbers and a scale.

## What you're looking at

This is the **International SVI-728**, starting in **MSX BASIC 1.0** with **28815 bytes free** and the `Ok` prompt. The figure reports the space BASIC makes available for programs and data, rather than the machine's total installed memory. No disk expansion is attached.

The exhibit uses the International keyboard layout. Visitors who remember a Swedish/Finnish SVI-728 may recognize the computer while finding a different arrangement of characters here. Interaction is through the keyboard.

### Try it

At `Ok`, type `PRINT 1+1` and press Enter. Open **Controls** and choose **Type in a demo program** to enter the short colour, text and music listing. Wait until the final `RUN` appears, then press Enter: it prints **HELLO SPECTRAVIDEO!**, lists the squares of 1 through 8 and plays a scale.

Type `LIST` to see how it works. Enter `40 FOR I=1 TO 10`, then `RUN`, to extend the table. A new line with an existing number replaces that line in memory.

**Restore to golden snapshot** returns to an empty BASIC prompt and discards your program. This exhibit runs offline.

## Legacy

The SVI-728 places Spectravideo within the first generation of MSX computers. The gallery's MSX2 and turbo R stations show later developments of that platform; this one preserves the earlier encounter with MSX BASIC. It also pairs naturally with the SV-328: similar-looking home computers on opposite sides of a change in software compatibility.
