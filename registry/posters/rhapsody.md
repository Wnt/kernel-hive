## Origins

In December 1996 Apple bought NeXT, and with it NeXTSTEP/OPENSTEP — a Mach kernel with a BSD personality, Display PostScript, and the Objective-C application frameworks — to become the foundation of its next operating system. The plan, code-named Rhapsody, was to put the Macintosh look on top of that NeXT system: the Platinum appearance of Mac OS 8, a Mac-style menu bar and Finder, over the NeXT Workspace, NetInfo, and a Unix shell. Developer Release 1 shipped in the autumn of 1997, seeded to some 10,000 developers; **Developer Release 2 (Rhapsody 5.1, May 1998)** — this station — was the last release seeded to developers before Apple renamed the shipping product Mac OS X Server 1.0 in 1999.

Like NeXTSTEP before it, Rhapsody was built for more than one processor. This is the **Intel** build, running on an ordinary PC of the period — the same 1998 hardware the NeXTSTEP 3.3 exhibit's Intel port would have run on — rather than a PowerPC Macintosh. Apple dropped the Intel version from the public roadmap after DR2; the next time it surfaced was as the secret Intel builds of Mac OS X, acknowledged at WWDC in 2005 and first shipped to the public in 2006.

## Significance

Rhapsody is the hinge between two systems shown elsewhere in this hall. Next to the NeXTSTEP station you can see what Apple kept — the Workspace, the Dock-like shelf, the installer, `bash` and Display PostScript underneath — and next to the Mac OS poster you can see what it added: the Platinum window chrome, the Apple menu, the Mac Finder. The "Yellow Box" (the NeXT frameworks, later Cocoa) and the "Blue Box" (a whole classic Mac OS running in a window for old applications) were the two halves of Apple's transition story: DR2 carried the Yellow Box on both PowerPC and Intel, while the PowerPC-only Blue Box had been shipping since the previous year as an add-on to DR1.

## What you're looking at

The Platinum Finder desktop over the NeXT Workspace at 1024×768 — Mac OS 8's grey menu bar, Finder windows and folder icons, drawn by Display PostScript on top of a Mach/BSD Unix. The application icons down the right edge (Mail, TextEdit, Preferences) and the vertical Workspace layout are pure NeXT; the menu bar, window chrome and Finder are the Mac face Apple grafted on. Open a Terminal from the Apple menu and you are in a Unix shell — `bash`, NetInfo, the whole NeXTSTEP substrate — under the Macintosh appearance.

## Legacy

Rhapsody became Mac OS X Server 1.0 (March 1999), still carrying Rhapsody's internal numbering as version 5.3, two steps beyond DR2's 5.1, and the first public release of what Apple would call Darwin. Then — with a new Aqua interface, Carbon for classic Mac applications, and the Cocoa name for the NeXT frameworks — it became Mac OS X 10.0 in 2001. Everything Apple ships today, from macOS to iOS, descends from the system on this screen.
