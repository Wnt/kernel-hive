# Sinclair ZX81 — gallery tile notes (udp/54128)

**Guest:** a captured **Debian 13 (trixie) x86_64 kiosk** running **MAME 0.289**
(`SUBTARGET=zx81`) emulating a **Sinclair ZX81** with the second-revision ROM
and the **1 KB** of memory the machine was sold with, resting at its own
untouched power-on screen. An **"emulator bridge"** tile — streamhost captures
the Linux framebuffer + AC97 audio exactly like every other tile. See
**`streamhost/docs/BRIDGE.md`**.

**Shared base:** `/data/vms/bridge/bridge-base.qcow2` (frozen, read-only).
**Build script (tile):** `scripts/build-guests/tiles/zx81.sh` — thin overlay + kiosk
`launch.sh` + romset assembly + quiet console + golden bake + a predicate
validated against its own negatives, fully automated.
**MAME build:** `scripts/build-guests/emulators/build-mame-zx81.sh`.
**Tile dir (host):** `/data/vms/streamhost/tiles/zx81/`.
**Registry entry:** `registry/tiles/zx81.json` (slot 128, udp 54128, VMID 231,
ssh hostfwd 127.0.0.1:5831).

## Media, and the licence position

One external file: **`zx81a.rom`**, the second-revision 8 KB ZX81 ROM
(sha1 `7b143ee964e9ada89d1f9e88f0bd48d919184cfc`, sha256
`14ad84f4243efcd41587ff46ab932d11087043e8d455a1ed2a227b9657828dfa`,
8 192 bytes), which is what almost every shipped ZX81 contained. Staged at
`/data/assets-staging/zx81/zx81a.rom`; the row is in
[`docs/lab/ASSETS-MANIFEST.md`](../lab/ASSETS-MANIFEST.md) and in
`scripts/build-guests/check-assets.sh`.

**The Amstrad permission does not apply here.** Amstrad's 1986 purchase of
Sinclair's computer business took the **Spectrum and QL** rights, and the
permission emulator authors rely on covers those ROMs. The ZX80/ZX81 ROM was
written by, and remains the copyright of, **Nine Tiles Networks Ltd**, who have
issued no equivalent grant. The ROM is therefore classed **preservation
source**: it is streamed as pixels, never committed to the repo, never served,
and there is no download affordance anywhere in the tile.

`-bios 3rd` (`zx81b.rom`, sha1 `c6d8e06cb936989f6e1cc7a56d1f092da854a515`) could
not be found in any preservation set that is reachable today. It is a later and
rarer revision and is not pursued; the tile pins `-bios 2nd` deliberately, not
as a fallback.

## The MAME binary, and how the romset was checked against it

The tile ships a **purpose-built MAME 0.289** (upstream commit
`f34f02505e32c1993c6a782b6814232cbfc74e36`, `SUBTARGET=zx81
SOURCES=src/mame/sinclair/zx.cpp`), built in the **trixie** chroot. That is the
same upstream commit `mpf2` pins, so the collection has one MAME provenance
rather than two. The two alternatives were rejected for stated reasons: the lab
host's packaged MAME is 0.276, and the guest suite's own package is older than
what is already in the collection — neither is a pin anyone chose, and the
romset here is only meaningful against the binary the tile ships.

Migrated bookworm → trixie on 2026-08-10 (wave 2 of
[`../lab/BRIDGE-TRIXIE-MIGRATION.md`](../lab/BRIDGE-TRIXIE-MIGRATION.md)). The
chroot used to exist because a host-built binary could not load in a Debian 12
guest; guest and host are the same generation now, so it survives for
reproducibility rather than for ABI.

**Unlike mpf2, no patch is applied.** mpf2 needs
`mame-irix-skip-warnings.patch` because its driver is marked imperfect and MAME
puts a full-screen red *"THIS SYSTEM DOESN'T WORK"* panel in front of it that
`-skip_gameinfo` does not suppress. `zx81` reports `status="good"`
`emulation="good"`, so it never raises that panel. The builder asserts that
status **from the shipped binary's own `-listxml`** and then proves it on the
framebuffer, because a headless probe never renders the panel and would pass
either way.

**`-verifyroms` is not the gate, and would be the wrong one.** The `zx81`
driver has five BIOS entries — 1st, 2nd and 3rd ROM revisions plus two
third-party Forth ROMs — and `-verifyroms` reports *bad* whenever any of them
is absent, however perfect the one that is pinned. The builder instead reads
the `(name, sha1)` pair belonging to `bios="2nd"` out of the shipped binary's
`-listxml`, refuses to continue unless that sha1 equals the staged ROM's,
assembles `zx81.zip` with exactly that member name, and re-hashes the member it
just wrote from inside the zip. Assembling by sha1 rather than by filename is
the rule the earlier MAME adds paid for: MAME renames rom members and moves
parent/clone splits between versions.

## Device set and geometry

QEMU is the standard bridge set (`pc-i440fx-11.0,vmport=off`, KVM, `-cpu host`,
2 vCPU, `-vga std`, dbus display + AC97, one IDE thin overlay, `-usb` with **no
tablet**, PS/2 keyboard only). RAM is **768 MB** rather than the 1536 MB the
VICE tiles use; the builder measures `MemAvailable` in the guest at the fixture
and fails below 200 MB, so a future MAME cannot silently outgrow it.

The kiosk launcher is `/etc/bridge/launch.sh`:

```
exec /opt/zx81/mame/zx81 zx81 \
  -bios 2nd -ramsize 1K \
  -rompath /opt/zx81/roms -inipath /opt/zx81 \
  -skip_gameinfo -video soft -prescale 2 -keepaspect -nowindow -nofilter
```

- **`-ramsize 1K`, not MAME's 16K default.** The ZX81 as sold had one kilobyte;
  the 16 KB RAM pack (and its wobble) belongs on the placard, not in the
  fixture. A visitor gets a little under 800 bytes for a program, which is the
  authentic experience and the reason `1 REM` mattered.
- **Fullscreen with aspect correction on the stock 1024×768 X root.** The
  driver's raster is 384×311 at 50.655 Hz; that is a pixel count, not a picture
  shape. MAME's aspect correction reconstructs the 4:3 television image, and
  1024×768 is exactly 4:3, so the picture fills the captured frame edge to edge.
  Forcing `-resolution 384x311` is the mpf2 trap: a strip in a black root.
- **No sound flags, and none needed.** The ZX81 has no sound hardware. AC97 is
  wired like every other bridge tile and carries silence by design.
- X runs `-nocursor`: keyboard-only exhibit, and a frozen core pointer in the
  middle of a nearly empty white screen would be the most visible thing on it.

## Readiness: why every sibling's predicate is wrong here

This is the interesting engineering problem in the tile, and it is worth
stating plainly, because both of the shapes already in the tree fail:

- `vic20.sh` requires **more than 100 000 bright pixels**. A ZX81 idle screen is
  a white field, so it passes — but so does a ZX81 that has been cleared, that
  has crashed with the display file intact, or that is **blanking its display
  because it is computing**, which is this machine's single most characteristic
  behaviour. The test accepts frames that are not the fixture, and a bake would
  keep one.
- `pdp11.sh` requires **more than 12 000 ink pixels**. The ZX81 fixture's entire
  ink content is *one character cell* — a few hundred pixels. Any threshold high
  enough to reject a black root rejects the real thing.

The predicate that ships is geometric, lives in
`streamhost/tiles/zx81/zx81-frame.py`, and has four clauses:

| Clause | Value at the fixture | The negative it exists for |
|---|---|---|
| `white ≥ 0.85` of the frame | ~0.99 | black X root, dead emulator, a MAME warning panel |
| `120 ≤ ink_box ≤ 1600` in the bottom-left cursor cell | ~300 | an empty white field: display off while computing, or a cleared screen |
| `ink_out ≤ 400` everywhere else | ~0 | a screen carrying typed text, a listing or a report code |
| two captures 3 s apart measure identically | stable | anything still moving; the ZX81's `K` cursor does not blink |

The cursor box is derived from the geometry (the 256×192 display area centred
in the 384×311 raster, MAME scaling that to fill the root, column 0 of the
input line) rather than hardcoded in root pixels, so it survives a change of X
root.

**The builder runs its own negatives.** `validate_predicate` kills MAME and
requires the bare X root to be rejected; synthesises the empty-white-field frame
from the accepted fixture by painting the cursor cell white and requires that
to be rejected; types `P` and requires the resulting `PRINT` keyword to be
rejected; and restores the golden and re-accepts the fixture between each. A
predicate nobody has watched fail is a predicate that cannot fail. The
blank-field negative is **synthetic and labelled as such**: reproducing it from
the keyboard needs a whole typed-in program, because in FAST mode the display
comes back whenever BASIC waits for a key — which is exactly when a keystroke
could arrive.

## The fixture

The golden is the machine's own untouched power-on screen: a white field with a
single inverse-video `K` in the bottom-left corner and **nothing else**. No
banner, no memory count, no copyright line — the ZX81 prints none.

There was no curated alternative to weigh here, and that is worth recording
alongside the Plus/4 lesson (whose golden rested inside a ROM office suite and
had to be re-baked). On a machine that prints nothing at startup, the power-on
screen is the *only* state that is unambiguously the machine's own rather than
something the previous visitor left behind.

`K` is not decoration: it is the machine reporting that it is in **keyword
mode**, where the next key pressed enters a whole BASIC word rather than a
letter. Pressing `P` puts `PRINT` on the screen complete, and the cursor changes
to `L`. That is the exhibit's first surprise and the reason the keyboard proof
walks exactly that route.

## Keyboard

Keyboard-only: `--pointer none --input-backend disabled`. The ZX81's only other
port was a cassette recorder.

`SH_KEY_MIN_HOLD_MS` / `SH_KEY_MIN_GAP_MS` — see
`streamhost/tiles/zx81/tile.env.fixture`. MAME samples the emulated keyboard
matrix once per emulated frame, and this driver's frame is 19.74 ms (50.655 Hz),
so the playbook's two-frame floor is 40/40. The measurement is in
[Measurements](#measurements) below.

The SPA's `zx81` keyboard profile carries what a visitor cannot otherwise find,
all of it SHIFT chords that MAME passes straight through: **RUBOUT** (SHIFT+0,
the only way to delete), **EDIT** (SHIFT+1), **BREAK** (SHIFT+SPACE), the cursor
keys (SHIFT+5/6/7/8) and **FUNCTION** (SHIFT+NEWLINE). It deliberately does
*not* ship plain arrow keys: the ZX81's cursor keys are the SHIFT chords, so
host arrows would be four dead buttons.

## Reset and cold boot

`resetMode=loadvm`, internal snapshot `golden`, no post-restore keys. A cold
boot reaches the same screen with zero input — the machine asks for nothing on
the way up — so the cold-boot arm in `scripts/coldboot/bootrec-tiles.conf` and
`scripts/coldboot/zx81-zero-input-prep.md` record a clean hand-off. That arm
uses `BR_DETECT_TIER=3` (fixed timer) on purpose: tier 1's change-fraction
detector settles as happily on a blanked screen as on the fixture.

## Measurements

Recorded during the add (2026-08-09), on the live tile unless stated.

**Geometry.** MAME's aspect correction fills the 1024×768 root exactly, as
predicted: the captured picture's white bounding box is the whole frame. The
ZX81's display area is **not** centred in its raster — measured from a headless
MAME snapshot, the 256×192 area sits at raster x=54, y=56 — and raster rows
304–309 are a **black blanking band that belongs to the picture** (2 304 dark
pixels natively, 15 360 scaled). Those two facts are why the predicate counts
ink inside the display area only; a naive "ink on the screen" test measures the
band and nothing else.

**The fixture, measured.** `white=0.9801`, `ink_box=319` (0.76 of a character
cell — the inverse `K`), `ink_out=0`, `ink_border=15360`, `cell_px=421.5`.
Identical on the cold boot, on the baked golden, after `loadvm golden`, after
the keyboard proof, and under the live `streamhost@zx81`.

**Key pacing — a WINDOW, not a floor, and the playbook's derivation is wrong
here.** Two emulated frames at 50.655 Hz would be 40/40. Measured on a clone
(`/data/vms/soltest/BUILD-zx81/clone`, thin copy of the tile overlay, same
device set) by typing a fixed 20-key line at each pacing and counting the
character cells the ZX81 actually painted:

| hold = gap | keys landed (of 20) | runs |
|---|---|---|
| 40 ms | 1 | 3, identical |
| 60 ms | 4–9 | 5 |
| 80 ms | 19–20 | 5 (one imperfect) |
| **100 ms** | **20 / 20** | **13 runs, 260 keys, zero losses** |
| 120 ms | 8–18 | 5 |
| 160 ms | 13 | 3 |
| 250 ms | 8–14 | 3 |

This machine loses keys held **too long** as well as too briefly. No other tile
in the collection behaves that way and the frame-period model does not predict
it; the ZX81 ROM's own keyboard debounce/repeat logic is the obvious suspect but
was **not** identified, so what is recorded is the measurement and not a theory.
The tile ships **100/100**. Do not "tidy" it back towards 40/40.

Two traps inside the measurement itself are worth repeating. First,
`scripts/dev/emu-key-pacing-bisect.py`'s frame-compare is unusable on this
machine as configured: its 40-character default line overruns what a 1 KB ZX81
will accept on one line, so the reference capture came back as a *blank screen*
and every trial "mismatched". Counting painted character cells against a
20-key line is the form that works. Second, a **freshly restored VM swallows
the first keystrokes**: the first `p` after `loadvm golden` left the framebuffer
untouched three times in a row, and the same key seconds later put `PRINT` on
the screen. The builder's first negative-case run misreported that as
`PREDICATE BROKEN`; it now warms the path and retries, and reports a keyboard
failure as a keyboard failure.

**The SHIFT chords, on the framebuffer** (clone, 100/100). `SHIFT+0` RUBOUT
deleted the whole `PRINT` token and returned the cursor to `K` (ink_out
625 → 0, ink_box 223 → 319). `SHIFT+NEWLINE` FUNCTION changed the cursor glyph
(ink_box 319 → 294). `SHIFT+SPACE` BREAK visibly changed the screen at rest
(ink_box 319 → 434). `SHIFT+5` (cursor left) and `SHIFT+1` (EDIT) produced **no
measurable change** and are therefore **not shipped** — not because they are
believed broken, but because the ZX81 draws its cursor as a block *between*
characters, so a cursor move leaves the frame's total ink identical and the
proof cannot tell "moved" from "ignored".

**Memory.** Guest `MemTotal` 725 456 kB, `MemAvailable` 423 036 kB at the
fixture with X + MAME running; MAME RSS 152 800 kB; host QEMU RSS 695 420 kB at
768 MB of guest RAM. The builder asserts guest `MemAvailable` > 200 MB.

**MAME.** `0.289 (mame0289)`, sha256
`0507c8ee24cbd6be79e1ba39ab2d285e52af6ba3cc6de503898eae2e387279e1`, 68 155 224
bytes, at `/data/vms/streamhost/assets/zx81/mame/zx81`. Its own `-listxml zx81`
reports `status="good"` and gives `zx81a.rom` sha1
`7b143ee964e9ada89d1f9e88f0bd48d919184cfc` for `bios="2nd"` — byte-identical to
the staged ROM, which is how the romset is gated.

## Rollback

The tile is self-contained: `/data/vms/streamhost/tiles/zx81/` (thin overlay on
the frozen shared base) plus `streamhost@zx81`. To retire it, stop the unit and
remove the tile directory; nothing else in the fleet references it. To rebuild
the fixture, `scripts/build-guests/tiles/zx81.sh --force` — but note that a launcher
or X-geometry change invalidates the golden, so the bake must be re-run or a
reset will restore the old layout and the fix will appear not to have worked.
