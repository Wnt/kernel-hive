## Origins

By the late 2000s Palm's original Palm OS had reached the end of its useful life. The company that had defined the PDA needed a modern smartphone platform and chose not to extend the old system again.

The answer was **webOS**, introduced with the Palm Pre in 2009. Its applications were built around web technologies, but the striking part for users was the interface: a gesture area beneath the screen, a launcher, universal search and a **card** view that turned running applications into visible objects.

Palm was trying to preserve what had made its earlier handhelds good — immediacy and small focused tasks — while rebuilding the software underneath for a networked smartphone.

## Significance

The card metaphor made multitasking understandable without a task manager. Press Home and the current application shrinks into a card. Swipe sideways to another one. Throw a card away and the application closes.

That interaction became webOS's visual identity and influenced later mobile interfaces. The system also treated information from multiple services as one thing through ideas such as **Synergy**, merging contacts and calendars instead of forcing users to think in terms of separate provider databases.

webOS belongs beside Palm OS because the contrast is the story. The first Palm system simplified the PDA around a stylus and synchronization. webOS starts again around continuous networking, gestures and several applications alive at once.

## What you're looking at

The intended exhibit is the preserved Palm/HP SDK virtual device running the original Luna interface. The best resting scene is the **card view**, with the launcher and quick-launch bar immediately accessible.

The browser pointer or touch input stands in for the Pre's touchscreen and gesture area. The experience should emphasize switching between cards, opening the launcher and dismissing an application rather than exploring developer tools.

## Legacy

HP acquired Palm in 2010 and eventually stopped making webOS phones and tablets. The software survived in other forms, most visibly as the basis of LG's television platform, but the original phone ecosystem disappeared.

The cards did not. Variations of the same visual model now appear across mobile operating systems. webOS is the short-lived system that made that model feel obvious first.
