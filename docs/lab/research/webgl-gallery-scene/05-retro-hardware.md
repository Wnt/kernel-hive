## 2. Recommended approach split

- **Download free CC-BY (attribute in a credits page):** C64, Amiga 500, Apple II, IBM 5150, VT100, 90s beige PC+CRT, early LCD, smartphone, iMac-era (with IP caveat). This covers ~10 of 12 archetypes at hero-acceptable quality today. Normalize everything through one Blender pass (scale to real-world dims, rename screen mesh, re-bake to a common texel density, glTF export with KHR_materials so `MeshPhysicalMaterial` maps cleanly).
- **Buy (2–3 items, <$100 total):** Jim Platt C64 if it becomes the hero shot (4K PBR, glTF-ready beats the free ones), the $29 TurboSquid 90s AST 486 set (Model M keyboard included), optionally a Fab Retro Eighties pack for room-filling clutter.
- **Model from scratch (agent-driven Blender):** SPARCstation pizza box + Sun GDM-style CRT, and the Atari ST if the scans prove too messy. Ground truth: Thingiverse STLs, [the-blueprints.com A500 vector drawing](https://www.the-blueprints.com/vectordrawings/show/22126/commodore_amiga_500_a500/) (they also have an [A1200](https://www.the-blueprints.com/vectordrawings/show/23562/amiga_a1200/)), spec-sheet dimensions (§3).
- **AI-generate (Midjourney → image-to-3D): background props only, not heroes.** 2026 tools ([Meshy 6, Tripo Smart Mesh, Hunyuan3D, TRELLIS 2 comparisons](https://www.3daistudio.com/3d-generator-ai-comparison-alternatives-guide/best-image-to-3d-tools-2026), [rundiffusion roundup](https://learn.rundiffusion.com/ai-3d-model-generators/)) now do PBR + quad retopo, and Hunyuan3D at 1536³ voxels handles hard surfaces reasonably — but crisp panel lines, readable badges, and per-key keyboards are exactly what image-to-3D still smears. For a museum where the camera gets close, generated meshes will read as "melted". Use Midjourney instead for *texture/mood reference sheets* and AI-3D for shelf clutter (boxed software, joysticks, manuals).

## 3. Dimensional ground truth for scratch modeling

- **IBM PC family:** [minuszerodegrees.net](https://minuszerodegrees.net/51xx_manuals.htm) — scanned IBM Technical References ([AUG81 PDF](https://www.minuszerodegrees.net/manuals/IBM_5150_Technical_Reference_6025005_AUG81.pdf)), part-level dimensions, photo sets; plus [DOS Days 5150 picture gallery](https://www.dosdays.co.uk/computers/IBM%20PC%20(5150)/pics.php).
- **VT100:** official specs in the [VT100 Technical Manual ch.1 on vt100.net](https://vt100.net/docs/vt100-tm/chapter1.html) — monitor 45.72 × 36.20 × 36.83 cm, keyboard 45.72 × 20.32 × 8.89 cm.
- **C64 breadbin:** Lemon64 measurement threads ([1](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=9292), [2](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=70151)) — ~404 × 216 × 75 mm, with fat/slim case variants.
- **Photo archives:** [Computer History Museum online collection](https://computerhistory.org/collections/) and [Centre for Computing History searchable archive](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/pages/590/Search-Our-Archive/) — multi-angle photos of every machine in the lineup; [the-blueprints.com](https://www.the-blueprints.com/) for true orthographic vectors.
- An agent-driven Blender session should work from: (1) spec-sheet bounding box → (2) orthographic photo/blueprint as background image → (3) STL silhouette check where available. Photogrammetry scans (comPVter, DHA) double as proportion validators.

## 4. CRT rendering recipe for three.js / r3f

Layered build, all techniques sourced:

1. **Separate screen mesh with real bulge.** Model the tube face as a shallow sphere section behind a bezel — don't fake curvature only in UV. The [unframework/threejs-crt-shader](https://github.com/unframework/threejs-crt-shader) experiment is the closest prior art: canvas content → GPU texture → **CRT effect injected into a custom MeshStandardMaterial's *emissive* channel**, so specular/lighting layers on top naturally. That "effect lives in emissive, lighting stays physical" split is the key architectural decision.
2. **Live OS feed as emissive texture.** VideoTexture/CanvasTexture from the stream ([r3f video-texture discussion](https://github.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber/discussions/786)); the drei [monitors sandbox](https://codesandbox.io/s/monitors-1guobo) and [RenderTexture demo](https://codesandbox.io/s/drei-rendertexture-0z8i2c) show multi-screen live surfaces in r3f.
3. **CRT shader on the emissive sample:** barrel-distort UVs, then scanlines + phosphor/aperture-grille mask + slight RGB convergence error. Steal parameters from the emulation world: [libretro CRT shader docs](https://docs.libretro.com/shader/crt/) (crt-geom = curvature+mask+scanlines cheap; [crt-lottes-fast.glsl](https://github.com/libretro/glsl-shaders/blob/master/crt/shaders/crt-lottes-fast.glsl) is public-domain Shadertoy-derived), [Filthy Pants mask taxonomy](http://filthypants.blogspot.com/2020/02/crt-shader-masks.html) (subpixel-correct mask LUTs), and [Xor's GM Shaders Mini: CRT](https://mini.gmshaders.com/p/gm-shaders-mini-crt) for a compact do-it-all GLSL walkthrough. Web-native references: [crt-fx](https://github.com/stefanlegg/crt-fx) (WebGL scanlines/mask/bloom/aberration) and [cool-retro-term-webgl](https://github.com/remojansen/cool-retro-term-webgl) (curvature, phosphor glow, burn-in, jitter — a full effect checklist in JS). Interactive three.js demo with sliders: [daenavan CRT shader](https://daenavan.github.io/crt-threejs/).
4. **Glass shell over the tube:** a second mesh 1–2 mm proud of the phosphor face, `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with low roughness, `clearcoat`, and an environment map so the room reflects in the dark glass; when the "machine is off", the emissive drops to 0 and the tinted (dark grey-green) glass + env reflection *is* the look. The [dev.to "Realistic 3D Monitor with Reflections and HTML Interface" r3f article](https://dev.to/blamsa0mine/building-a-realistic-3d-monitor-with-reflections-and-html-interface-using-react-three-fiber-4dcj) covers the reflection layering.
5. **Glow spill onto bezel/desk:** selective bloom on the emissive screen only (threshold traps documented in [three.js#24703](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/24703) and [forum selective-bloom threads](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/selectivebloom-blooming-the-whole-model-emissive-texture/33679)) + a cheap trick bloom can't do: one dim PointLight/RectAreaLight in front of the screen whose color is the average of the current video frame (downsample the texture to 1×1 each few frames). That sells "live phosphor lighting the room".
6. **Performance:** one CRT shader material instance shared across stations, pause video textures + drop emissive when off-camera — consistent with your existing auto-pause architecture.

## 5. Realism detail checklist

- **Keyboards:** per-key geometry for hero machines — keys affect silhouette and self-shadow, which normal maps cannot ([normal-map limits explainer](https://customuse.com/learn/what-is-a-normal-map)); instance one keycap mesh per row-profile to keep cost trivial; legends as texture. Normal-mapped decal keyboards only for background units. The $29 TurboSquid set includes a real Model M if you'd rather buy one.
- **Cables:** `CatmullRomCurve3`/catenary points → [`TubeGeometry`](https://threejs.org/docs/#api/en/geometries/TubeGeometry) (see [tube-geometry guide](https://dustinpfister.github.io/2023/06/02/threejs-tube-geometry/), [three-tube-path](https://github.com/erasta/three-tube-path) for segment economy; [catenary math](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/engineering-calculators/cable-tension-calculator-catenary-and-parabolic)). A sagging power cord + coiled keyboard cable is the single cheapest realism add.
- **Wear & yellowing:** uneven "retrobright-failed" yellowing is UV-exposure-driven ([Retrobright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrobright)) — so gradient it: tops/rear vents yellow more than shielded areas, mismatched keycaps/panels are period-authentic. Edge-wear masks + fingerprint gloss variation via Substance/Blender ([80.lv retro-scene texturing breakdown](https://80.lv/articles/creating-retro-scene-with-blender-zbrush-substance-3d-painter)); base plastics from [Poliigon](https://www.poliigon.com/textures/plastic).
- **Stickers/badges/labels:** serial labels, "Intel Inside", inventory asset tags, floppy-drive scribbles — as decal planes or a decal atlas; peeling corners on one or two.
- **Ports/vents/feet:** rear I/O panels normal-mapped, but keep real geometry for anything in silhouette (carry handles, tilt feet, vent slots on top surfaces).
- **Dust:** a subtle top-facing dust layer (world-space-up mask in the shader or an AO-tinted sheen) on horizontal surfaces.

## 6. Trademark / trade-dress considerations

- **Baseline law (US):** under Rogers/realism doctrine, using real marks in an *expressive* work is protected when artistically relevant and not explicitly misleading; courts have accepted "realism" as artistic relevance ([Mondaq: trademarks in video games & the realism defense](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/trademark/1524968/the-use-of-trademarks-in-video-games-does-realism-serve-as-a-defense-to-infringement), [Snell & Wilmer trademarks-101 for games](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/intellectual-property-issues-for-game-developers-and-operators-part-1-trademarks-101-names-logos-trade-dress-and-protecting-a-brand-before-launch/)). A non-commercial "living computer museum" with historical framing is close to the best-case fact pattern.
- **Apple is the outlier:** it litigated the iMac G3 *shape itself* as trade dress and won worldwide injunctions ([Apple v. eMachines/Daewoo press release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2000/03/08Apple-Secures-Worldwide-Injunctions-Against-Daewoo-and-eMachines/), [eOne history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMachines_eOne)), and publishes strict [third-party guidelines](https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/guidelinesfor3rdparties.html). That precedent targeted competing *products*, not depictions — but if you genericize anything, genericize the Apple items (logo off, neutral gumdrop-translucent "all-in-one").
- **Community practice:** branded C64/Amiga/IBM models are ubiquitous and openly CC-licensed on Sketchfab (every find above) with no visible takedown pattern; Commodore/Atari marks have passed through lenient successor owners. Pragmatic posture for a public gallery: keep the *shapes* (that's the museum), drop or blur *logos/wordmarks* on Apple items, add a "all trademarks property of their owners; shown for historical documentation" credits page, and attribute every CC-BY model there (required by the license anyway).
